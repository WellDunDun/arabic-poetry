# Close reading and interpretation

Use for explaining a poem, critiquing its literary effect, or preserving meaning during revision. Read only as much historical context as the question needs. [Sources](SOURCES.md) distinguish original criticism, scholarly evaluation, and the workflow proposed here.

## Establish the literal reading

- Preserve the wording and meaningful variants. Identify who speaks, who is addressed, what happens, and the emotional or argumentative situation. The speaker is not automatically the historical poet.
- Resolve difficult vocabulary through context and an appropriate Arabic lexical source when needed. Explain the word's sense here; a root meaning alone may not explain an idiom or historical usage.
- Examine subject and predicate, subject and object, negation, conditional clauses, modifiers, and pronoun references. Al-Jurjani's discussion of نظم treats these relations as meaning-making choices; added vocalization can change an interpretation.
- Separate a verified occasion or allusion from a plausible context. Meter, theme, familiar diction, or a model's era prediction does not prove attribution. Say when an occasion is unknown rather than fill an explanation template with invented biography.

## Move from wording to interpretation

For a major claim, use this chain:

| Textual detail | Feature | Supported effect or interpretation | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A short exact phrase | Syntax, image, recurrence, contrast, sound, or break | What it makes the reader infer or experience here | Alternative reading or missing context, when consequential |

Explain the relation between these columns in prose; do not force a table into a simple answer. This adapts a community close-reading skill's evidence chain and the dimensions of the Fann or Flop evaluation, not an official journal rule or an aesthetic scoring system.

- For تشبيه or استعارة, identify what is connected and the feature supporting that connection. Explain what the image reveals, magnifies, compresses, or questions. A label without a reading of its effect adds little.
- For كناية, explain the implied quality and the context supporting it. Literal and implied readings may coexist. Do not force every image into exactly one rhetorical label.
- For تقديم وتأخير، حذف، استفهام، نفي or a shift in address, explain the change in emphasis or relation. Identify purposeful ambiguity before offering to clarify it away.
- For جناس، تكرار، توازٍ or recurring sounds, identify their role in meaning, continuity, irony, insistence, or pacing. Al-Jurjani criticizes ornament that distorts meaning; this does not justify banning deliberately patterned language.
- Check the neighboring lines and details that resist the interpretation. Distinguish explicit statements, supported implications, and speculation. Offer a second reading only when a real textual ambiguity warrants it.

## Read at the scale of the poem

- Trace how images or arguments develop, how the addressee changes, and how the opening relates to the ending. A poem may intentionally use discontinuity or juxtaposition. It does not need a mandatory narrative arc or revelation.
- Examine whether a repeated phrase changes meaning through its new context. Redundancy and refrain require different editorial responses.
- Recognize organizing devices such as dialogue, a central image, or changing recurrence without treating one as obligatory. Asal Hasan's study of Onsi al-Haj provides such readings; it does not define every prose poem as short or prove that repetition itself gives literary value.
- In تفعيلة or prose poetry, compare a passage with an alternative line break or continuous prose as a temporary experiment. Identify any change in suspension, emphasis, pace, or ambiguity. This tests the effect of those breaks; it does not classify the poem's meter or determine whether it deserves to be called poetry.
- For a موشح, map repeated أقفال and varying أبيات according to the intended model. Preserve that scheme when interpreting repetition or judging rhyme. A short excerpt may not reveal it.

## Give usable feedback

For critique, select the findings that matter to the request. Quote the relevant passage, explain the effect, and suggest a bounded revision or reading experiment. Describe what the edit gains and sacrifices. Do not claim every compression is an improvement or remove symbolic language simply because it is abstract.

For explanation, give an accessible paraphrase first when helpful, then the interpretive detail that the paraphrase loses. Keep historical explanation, formal analysis, and personal response distinguishable.

Review these dimensions independently:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Request adherence | Did the result meet the form, tone, length, dialect, and editing scope? |
| Technical form | Where meter/rhyme is intended, is the actual pronounced sequence valid? |
| Language and meaning | Is the reading grammatically plausible, and did the revision preserve the intended proposition? |
| Literary effect | What specific images, relations, sounds, and developments make the poem effective or weak? |
| Evidence | Which claims follow from text, which require outside sources, and which remain interpretations? |

Do not collapse these into an unsupported percentage or use an agent's self-review as evidence of human literary acceptance.
