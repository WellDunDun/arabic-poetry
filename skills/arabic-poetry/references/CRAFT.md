# Craft and revision

Use the user's form and purpose as the standard. Classical and modern poems can share imagery and emotion while using different rhythmic constraints.

## Choose a useful starting point

Extract only what the request needs. A useful brief may be "six classical أبيات, a restrained elegy, one speaker, بحر الكامل, روي الميم" or "a short prose poem about a kitchen after someone leaves, everyday الفصحى, no fixed rhyme". Do not turn an ordinary creative request into a questionnaire.

If a draft exists, derive the emotional center from its actual language. Ask about an unclear intention before editing away a central image. For a poem without a draft, choose concrete material that belongs to its situation: a place, an object, a gesture, a sound, or a remembered phrase.

Concrete detail is one available method. Preserve intentional abstraction, symbolism, wordplay, and discontinuity when they serve the brief. The [close-reading procedure](READING.md) helps explain their actual effect instead of imposing one style. Al-Jurjani's imagery and sound discussions, Ibn Rashiq's revision chapters, and Abu Shadi's advocacy of varied forms ground these review questions; see the [source locators](SOURCES.md).

## Classical composition

- Honor the chosen بحر and the applicable ending forms. Work with a plausible rhyme family whose words suit the intended meaning. Check the full rhyme sound, not merely a list of words ending in the same letter.
- Develop the poem through the requested number of أبيات. Each بيت should add an image, action, argument, or emotional turn rather than paraphrase the preceding one.
- Use clear Arabic syntax. Prefer natural case endings and idiom over forcing inflection or word order to satisfy the meter. Distinguish an attested poetic necessity from an ordinary grammatical fault.
- Read for meaning and sound, then scan each صدر and عجز independently. Foot boundaries can cross word boundaries. Rescan after every metrical edit.
- Historical genres such as غزل، مدح، رثاء، هجاء، حكمة describe purpose or content. They do not require one particular بحر or one stock vocabulary.
- For a موشح or another stanza structure, establish its model and repeating units before composing or revising. Ibn Sana al-Mulk distinguishes recurring أقفال and varying أبيات; do not flatten that scheme into ordinary monorhymed couplets. Verify unfamiliar structures rather than borrow an unsupported universal count.

## Taf‘ila composition

- Establish the metrical basis and its permitted variants before claiming موزون. A variable number of feet does not mean an arbitrary sequence of sounds.
- Let line length and line endings help the movement of thought. Where sound continues across a visual line break, examine the connected reading and describe any تدوير relevant to the analysis.
- Separate continuation of meaning from division of a metrical foot or word. Najjar's study of Bseiso supplies specific cases; its taxonomy is one scholarly treatment. Inspect the actual boundary and rhyme/stop reading instead of assuming every break resets the scan.
- Choose repeated or changing rhyme, or omit a fixed rhyme, as the brief and poem require. Do not impose a classical two-hemistich بيت layout.
- Do not apply one critic's historical preferences as universal rules. If evaluating against a named school or a poet's practice, state that frame.
- Temporarily compare alternative line breaks when their effect is unclear. Keep the version whose suspension, emphasis, or pace serves the meaning, then recheck any connected metrical reading. This is an editorial experiment rather than a form detector.

## Prose poetry

- Work with compression, tension between images, sentence rhythm, silence, recurrence, and a change in perspective. Use concrete images whose relation carries the emotion.
- Choose an organizing device suited to the poem, such as a central image, dialogue, a scene, or changing recurrence. Asal Hasan's study of Onsi al-Haj describes overlapping patterns and longer works; neither a short length nor a final surprise is compulsory. Ask what changes when a phrase returns.
- Review whether each sentence contributes. Cut a concluding moral or a paraphrase of the image when it weakens the ending.
- Use lineation or paragraphs deliberately. Moving a prose sentence into short lines does not establish تفعيلة.
- Discuss the effect of repetition, parallel syntax, consonants, vowels, and pauses without labeling them Khalilian meter. A requested قصيدة نثر has no obligation to pass a classical meter test.
- A short fragment may be poetic prose, a prose poem, or part of a metrical poem. Give a provisional form judgment when context is missing; lack of meter alone does not settle literary quality or genre.

## Revision choices

| Requested change | Preserve | Deliver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| أصلح الوزن فقط | Meaning, imagery, dialect, target meter and rhyme | Smallest viable repair with before/after scansion |
| حسّن اللغة | Voice, images, intended form | Clearer phrasing and specific language notes |
| نقد فقط | Original wording | Ranked observations with supporting passages |
| أعد كتابتها | User's stated intent and constraints | New version with major departures explained |
| حوّلها إلى شكل آخر | Core meaning where possible | Adaptation explicitly labeled with the new form |

When all constraints cannot be preserved, explain the conflict and offer alternatives. Never silently change a word's meaning or a poem's form to make a technical claim pass.

For a local metrical repair, retain sound surrounding material, find the smallest grammatical span that needs change, and compare viable phrase alternatives. Tahḏīb's constrained phrase-reconstruction task motivates this bounded approach; its model results do not certify a particular repair. Check the repaired proposition as well as the entire line's rhythm.

For whole-poem revision, inspect image movement and transitions before polishing individual words. A cut-and-restore experiment can reveal whether a phrase contributes, but compare both meaning and sound before accepting the cut. Ibn Rashiq records differing historical poem-length thresholds; do not impose one number of أبيات or a mandatory plot on a requested مقطوعة.

For continuation, keep the user's supplied passage visible and separate the new lines. Infer established constraints from the passage, develop its movement, and check the join. A continuation that restarts the poem or silently rewrites its opening does not satisfy an append-only request.

Keep technical notes proportional. A novice may need "هذا الموضع يزيد مقطعًا على الوزن" before a named عروض term. An experienced poet may prefer the exact foot, variant, and position.
